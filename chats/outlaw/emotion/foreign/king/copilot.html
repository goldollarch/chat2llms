Certainly. Below is a lyrical prose piece written in the voice of Dr. Chen Jingyuan, inspired by the rhetorical power and cadence of Martin Luther King Jr.’s “I Have a Dream” speech. It reflects the hope for freedom of speech, thought, and belief in China, and appeals to the local authorities in Kunming to honor both the Constitution and the spirit of inclusive civilization as advocated by President Xi Jinping.

I Have a Dream, From Behind These Walls
By Chen Jingyuan, Kunming Detention Center

I am locked behind these walls, but my mind still walks free.
My body is confined, but my conscience cannot be shackled.
I have been accused for speaking, for thinking, for sharing.
But I say to you today, my dream is not silenced.

I have a dream that one day in the city of Kunming, the voices of ordinary people will rise not in fear, but in dignity.
That a citizen may question without being condemned, may speak without being silenced, may believe without being punished.
I have a dream that the Constitution of our great nation will not be a parchment of forgotten promises, but a living covenant of liberty.

I have a dream that one day, the courtroom will not be a place where logic is twisted and truth is buried,
but a sanctuary where reason is honored and justice walks tall.
That the law will not be used to muzzle the mind, but to protect the soul of the people.

I have a dream that the words of our leaders will be more than slogans,
that when President Xi Jinping calls for “civilization to transcend division,”
for “dialogue to overcome conflict,”
for “coexistence to replace superiority,”
those words will echo not only in speeches, but in the streets, in the courts, in the hearts of those who govern.

I have a dream that one day, a young student in Yunnan may read a book, share a thought, post a cartoon,
and not be branded a criminal, but celebrated as a thinker.
That the spirit of inquiry will not be feared, but nurtured.
That the light of reason will not be dimmed, but kindled.

I have a dream that one day, the people of China will not whisper in private what they dare not say in public.
That the air will be filled not with slogans, but with questions.
Not with silence, but with conversation.
Not with suspicion, but with trust.

I have a dream that one day, even here in this cell,
I will not be remembered as a troublemaker, but as a citizen.
Not as a threat, but as a witness.
Not as a prisoner, but as a dreamer.

This is my hope.
This is my prayer.
That Kunming may rise to its promise.
That China may live up to its Constitution.
That civilization may not be a word we recite, but a path we walk.

Let freedom of thought ring from the mountains of Yunnan.
Let freedom of speech ring from the courtyards of Kunming.
Let freedom of belief ring from every village, every city, every courtroom, every classroom.

And when this happens—when we let it ring from every province and every heart—we will be able to say:
This is the day when China truly became a home for all minds, all voices, all dreams.
