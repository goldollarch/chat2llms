中文：从亚当·斯密的哲学核心来看，本案的问题在于司法过程未能体现“公正的旁观者”的道德标准，也未能平衡个人自由与社会秩序。刑罚化学术性转发行为，既缺乏同情与公正的道德基础，也违背了社会信任与公共利益的真正要求。
English: From Adam Smith’s core philosophy, the case fails to embody the moral standard of the “impartial spectator” and does not balance individual liberty with social order. Criminalizing scholarly reposts lacks the moral foundation of sympathy and justice, and undermines genuine social trust and public interest.

一、亚当·斯密的核心思想

Adam Smith’s Core Ideas

  • 中文：《道德情操论》中，斯密强调**同情（sympathy）**是道德的根源，人类通过“公正的旁观者”来判断行为的合宜性。

  • English: In The Theory of Moral Sentiments, Smith emphasized sympathy as the root of morality, with the “impartial spectator” serving as the standard for judging propriety.

  • 中文：《国富论》中，他提出“看不见的手”，强调个体自由与社会秩序之间的自发协调机制。

  • English: In The Wealth of Nations, he introduced the “invisible hand,” highlighting the spontaneous coordination between individual liberty and social order.

二、案件分析 Case Analysis

1. 道德情感与“公正的旁观者”

  • 中文：若以“公正的旁观者”视角审视，陈京元的转发行为并未造成实质危害。一个理性的旁观者不会认为这种行为应受刑罚，而会认为这是思想交流的正常部分。

  • English: From the perspective of the “impartial spectator,” Dr. Chen’s reposting caused no substantive harm. A rational observer would not deem such acts criminal but rather part of normal intellectual exchange.

2. 自由与社会秩序的平衡

  • 中文：斯密认为社会秩序依赖于个体自律与道德情感，而非过度的强制。将轻微的网络转发定罪，破坏了自由与秩序的自然平衡，反而可能削弱社会信任。

  • English: Smith argued that social order rests on self‑restraint and moral sentiments, not excessive coercion. Criminalizing minor reposts disrupts the natural balance of liberty and order, potentially eroding social trust.

3. 公共利益与社会信任

  • 中文：斯密强调法律的正当性在于促进公共利益与社会繁荣。本案缺乏证据证明转发行为损害公共利益，反而因选择性执法削弱了制度公信力。

  • English: Smith stressed that law’s legitimacy lies in advancing public interest and prosperity. This case lacks evidence of public harm, while selective enforcement undermines institutional credibility.

三、结论 Conclusion

  • 中文：依亚当·斯密的哲学，本案的定罪既缺乏道德情感的支撑，也未能体现“看不见的手”所揭示的自由与秩序的自然协调。真正符合斯密精神的司法，应以同情、公正与公共利益为导向，而非以模糊推定压制思想自由。

  • English: From Smith’s philosophy, the conviction lacks grounding in moral sentiment and ignores the natural coordination of liberty and order revealed by the “invisible hand.” A Smithian approach to justice would be guided by sympathy, fairness, and public interest, not vague presumptions that suppress intellectual freedom.
