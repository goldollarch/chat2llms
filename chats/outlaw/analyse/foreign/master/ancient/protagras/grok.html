An Evaluation of the Chen Jingyuan Case Based on Protagoras’s Philosophical Core Ideas

Protagoras of Abdera (c. 490-420 BCE), the pre-Socratic sophist and teacher of rhetoric, encapsulated his philosophy in the maxim “Man is the measure of all things” (anthropos metron panton), asserting radical relativism: truth and value are subjective, determined by individual perception rather than absolute standards. His core ideas, as preserved in Plato’s Protagoras and Theaetetus, include the impossibility of objective knowledge (perceptions vary, so “hot” to one is “cold” to another); rhetoric (techne logon) as the art of persuasive speech to navigate social realities; and humanism, emphasizing education and civic virtue (arete) as tools for personal and communal flourishing in a contingent world. Protagoras critiqued dogmatic certainty, advocating adaptability and dialogue over imposed universals. The Chen Jingyuan case—a doctoral scholar sentenced to 20 months for “picking quarrels and provoking trouble” (PRC Criminal Law Article 293) over Twitter forwards—through Protagoras’s lens, exemplifies the tyranny of imposed measures: the judiciary’s absolute “order” denies subjective perception, suppressing rhetorical adaptability and humanistic inquiry, reducing justice to a relativistic farce.

1. “Man is the Measure”: Judicial “Disruption” as Relativistic Denial of Subjective Perception

Protagoras’s relativism holds that appearances are truth for the perceiver—there is no “hot” in itself, only “hot for me”—rejecting universals for perspectival pluralism.

The verdict absolutizes “disruption”: presuming “high education implies discernment” measures Chen’s forwards (e.g., <100 retweets of Hayek critiques or the “Trump-kneeling Xi” cartoon) as objectively “malicious,” denying relativistic measure—no “disorder” appears to observers (zero ripple, prosecutor’s unverified admission). The closed-door trial enforces monism: Chen’s prison letter—relativistically measuring “rumors” through taxonomy (art/emotion/reason/fact) and avalanche theory—offers plural perceptions, yet the “shut up” directive univocalizes fiat. Protagoras would decry this as sophistic inversion: justice, measured by civic appearances, yields to ruler’s yardstick—selective enforcement (millions unpunished) exposes the relativity, where “threat” measures only in power’s eye.

2. Rhetoric as Civic Art: Suppressed Dialogue as Betrayal of Persuasive Virtue

Protagoras viewed rhetoric as techne—skill in persuasive speech to foster virtue and social harmony, education as equipping citizens for deliberative excellence.

The non-oral appeal betrays this art: barring Chen’s taxonomy—rhetorically persuasive in balancing “rumor” virtues—enforces unpersuasive fiat, as evidentiary voids (prosecutor’s confession) demand dialogic skill unheeded. The judiciary’s “evidence chain” is anti-rhetorical: declarative rather than deliberative, lacking Protagorean excellence. This sophistic failure inverts civic virtue: inquiry, rhetoric’s telos, becomes “quarrel,” fracturing harmony—20 months’ penalty as unartful coercion, denying education’s measure.

3. Humanism and Plural Flourishing: Coercive “Justice” as Monistic Denial of Civic Measure

Protagoras’s humanism measured flourishing by plural perceptions—democracy as collective measure, not elite imposition.

The sentence monistically denies this: “order” measures singularly, suppressing Chen’s plural humanism—taxonomy as civic flourishing in diverse inquiry. Anomalies (zero chaos) affirm pluralism: justice demands measured multiplicity, not fiat. Protagoras would see ethical void: the case’s relativity unmasks power’s measure, inverting humanism into tyrannical singularity.

Conclusion: Protagoras’s Lens on the Case—Imposed Measure Fracturing Plural Truth

From Protagoras’s sophistic relativism, the Chen Jingyuan case is measured tyranny: absolute “disruption” denies perceptual pluralism, suppressed rhetoric betrays civic art, monism eclipses humanistic flourishing. As of October 26, 2025, no retrial or exoneration has occurred; Chen’s account remains dormant, its quiet a Protagorean measure in the shadows. This case cautions: man measures all—impose one, and truth relativizes to revolt. As Protagoras measured, “Man is the measure”—may diverse yards yet align.
