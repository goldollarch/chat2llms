A Heideggerian Analysis of Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s Case: Authenticity, the Call of Conscience, and the Inauthentic “They”

Martin Heidegger’s ontology in Being and Time (1927) centers on Dasein—human existence as “being-there”—which is primordially authentic (eigentlich) when it owns its finitude and possibilities, but typically falls into inauthenticity (Uneigentlichkeit) through absorption in the “they” (das Man): the anonymous, conformist chatter of everydayness that evades individual responsibility. Authenticity emerges via anxiety (Angst), revealing the “nothing” of thrownness (Geworfenheit)—our arbitrary insertion into the world—and summoning the call of conscience (Ruf des Gewissens), a silent, individualized appeal to resolutely (Entschlossenheit) seize one’s “ownmost potentiality-for-Being” (eigenste Seinkönnen). This is not heroic isolation but a resolute care (Sorge) for Being, confronting death as the ultimate horizon. Heidegger critiques modern “idle talk” (Gerede) and “curiosity” (Neugier) as distractions from this disclosure. Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s 2023 conviction for “picking quarrels and provoking trouble” (寻衅滋事罪)—for forwarding low-engagement Twitter posts—dramatizes this tension: a scholar’s authentic inquiry drowned in the inauthentic “they” of bureaucratic conformity, yet his prison writings evoke a Heideggerian call to resoluteness.

Chen’s everyday engagement—sharing posts on his negligible account (@_cenjoy) about art (e.g., the “umbrella girl” cartoon as symbolic disclosure of resistance), emotion (e.g., June 4th candlelight as mood-revealing attunement), theory (e.g., political spectrum analyses, Trump’s critique of communism, Pompeo’s U.S.-China remarks as questioning technological enframing), and history (e.g., Mao’s Selected Works edits, Deng’s retirement endorsement as historical sending)—exemplifies authentic Dasein in its care-structure. With zero followers and under 100 reposts, these were not public spectacles but private “world-disclosures” (Weltenthüllung), Heidegger’s term for how equipmental dealings reveal Being. As a physicist of complex systems, Chen’s forwards enact resoluteness: confronting the “thrownness” of global discourses, he anticipates his ownmost possibility—scholarly truth-seeking—without fleeing into the “they’s” safe chatter. Heidegger would see this as anticipatory resoluteness (Vorlaufen), owning finitude by engaging life’s multiplicities amid indifference.

The Kunming judiciary—Judge Pu Huijun, Prosecutor Ge Bin, and appellate Judge Li Xiangyun—embodies the “they”: lost in das Man, they “sort” Chen’s posts as “false information” disrupting “order,” a fallenness (Verfallen) into idle talk that levels all to conformity. The presumption of “high education = malice” is pure inauthenticity: evading the call of conscience, they objectify Chen as an “in-the-world” threat (Zuhandenheit), reducing his freedom to a calculable “disorder.” Procedural absurdities—non-public trial, denied defenses, suppressed prison letter—reveal anxiety’s evasion: the “they” flees thrownness by enforcing a homogenized world-picture, where art becomes “rumors,” theory “insults,” history “fabrications.” Heidegger’s critique of “publicness” (Öffentlichkeit) fits: the law here is Gerede, chattering about “national security” without disclosing Being, a “levelling down” that silences individual care.

Yet, Chen’s Prison Blood Letter—vowing “life without end, struggle without cease” and lifelong accountability—resonates as the call of conscience: amid anxiety’s uncanniness (Unheimlichkeit), he hears the silent summons to authenticity. Facing iron bars as his “being-towards-death” (Sein-zum-Tode), Chen resolutely chooses his ownmost: not victimhood, but defiant inquiry (Gödel’s limits as thrownness’s disclosure). His exposure of selective enforcement (state media unscathed) and fabricated “disorder” (zero impact) anticipates resoluteness, pulling Dasein from the “they” toward care. Heidegger’s “uncanny” homecoming applies: prison, the ultimate unhomeliness, becomes the site of authentic self-appropriation.

Heideggerian analysis thus frames Chen’s case as a micro-drama of authenticity’s strife: the “they’s” fallen world crushes resolute Dasein, yet the call persists. In modernity’s enframed forgetfulness of Being, Chen’s unyielding scholarship reminds us: authenticity demands vigilant care, not conformity. His revolt—eternal in its finitude—discloses the judicial “they” as the true abyss, urging a return to disclosed existence.
