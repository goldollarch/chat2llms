From Alain Badiou’s philosophical perspective, the case of Dr. Chen Jingyuan exemplifies a rare fidelity to truth in the face of state power. His silence is not mere resistance—it is a militant affirmation of a truth-event that ruptures the prevailing order. In refusing to confess, Dr. Chen enacts a subjectivation that aligns with Badiou’s ethics: “to remain faithful to the event.”

1. Truth-Event: The Irruption of Justice into the Legal Void

Badiou defines a truth-event as a rupture in the situation—a moment that cannot be accounted for by the dominant order. Dr. Chen’s refusal to confess, his silence under coercion, is such an event. It interrupts the state’s legal narrative, which seeks to reduce dissent to criminality.

His silence is not passive—it is an affirmation of a truth that the regime cannot name. It is the irruption of justice into a field governed by procedural falsity.

2. Subjectivation: Becoming a Subject of Truth

In Badiou’s framework, a subject is not a pre-given identity but a process—a fidelity to a truth-event. Dr. Chen becomes a subject not by asserting his rights within the system, but by refusing to be interpolated by its categories. He does not accept the name “criminal,” nor the logic of confession.

His silence is a subjective act, a militant fidelity to the truth of injustice. He does not speak because the language offered to him is false. In this refusal, he becomes a subject of truth.

3. Ethics of Fidelity: “Do Not Betray the Event”

Badiou’s ethics are not based on abstract principles but on fidelity: the commitment to a truth that emerges from an event. Dr. Chen’s case is not just a legal matter—it is an ethical drama. His silence is a refusal to betray the event of injustice he has witnessed and endured.

He does not compromise, does not negotiate, does not recant. This fidelity is not stubbornness—it is ethical clarity, a refusal to let truth be buried beneath the machinery of power.

4. The State as Simulacrum of Law

Badiou distinguishes between genuine law and the state’s simulacrum of legality. When law becomes a tool of repression, it ceases to be law in the true sense—it becomes a mask for power. Dr. Chen’s case exposes this simulacrum: the charge of “picking quarrels” is not law, but a fiction that serves domination.

His silence unmasks this fiction. It reveals that the law, in this case, is not a universal procedure but a partisan weapon.

5. Conclusion: Fidelity in the Void

From Alain Badiou’s philosophical standpoint, Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s silence is a heroic fidelity to truth. It is a refusal to betray the event of injustice, a rejection of false names, and a militant affirmation of subjectivity. In a world saturated with simulacra, his silence is a real act, a gesture that reconfigures the coordinates of ethics and politics.

“The only ethics is the ethics of truth. The only subject is the subject of fidelity.” — Alain Badiou

Dr. Chen’s silence is not absence—it is fidelity. It is not weakness—it is strength. It is not defeat—it is the beginning of a new political truth.
