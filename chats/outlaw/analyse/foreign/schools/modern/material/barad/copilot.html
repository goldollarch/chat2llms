From Karen Barad’s New Materialist philosophy—especially her theory of agential realism—Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s case reveals how political injustice is not merely a human drama, but a material-discursive entanglement where silence, law, and resistance intra-act to produce ethical ruptures. His refusal to confess is not absence, but a performative cut that reconfigures the field of power.

1. Intra-action: Ethics Emerges from Entanglement

Barad’s concept of intra-action rejects the idea of separate entities interacting externally. Instead, entities emerge through their entanglements. Dr. Chen’s silence, the courtroom, the legal discourse, the prison architecture, and the public memory are not separate—they co-constitute the event.

His refusal to speak is not a withdrawal from the system—it is an agential act within it, a reconfiguration of the material-discursive field that forces the apparatus to confront its own instability.

2. The Performative Cut: Refusal as Ontological Intervention

Barad argues that every measurement, every boundary, is a cut—a performative act that enacts reality. Dr. Chen’s refusal to confess is such a cut. It does not merely reject the state’s narrative; it enacts a different ontology, one where truth is not dictated by power, and where ethical responsibility cannot be coerced.

This cut is not symbolic—it is material. It alters the conditions of possibility for justice, memory, and resistance.

3. Matter Speaks: Silence as Material Agency

Barad insists that matter is not passive—it “feels, converses, suffers, desires, yearns and remembers”. Dr. Chen’s silence is not emptiness—it is matter speaking. His body, his presence, his refusal—all exert force within the legal assemblage.

His silence is a material protest, a refusal to be reduced to a discursive object. It is an ethical stance that emerges from the entangled agencies of conscience, law, and history.

4. Responsibility Without Separation

Agential realism holds that ethics is not based on autonomous subjects making choices, but on responsibility within entanglement. Dr. Chen does not stand outside the system judging it—he is within it, and his refusal is a response to the entangled violence it enacts.

His silence is not a claim to purity—it is a response-ability, a way of being accountable to the histories, bodies, and truths that the system seeks to erase.

5. Conclusion: Silence as Ethical Entanglement

From Karen Barad’s New Materialist perspective, Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s case is not a clash between individual and state—it is a material reconfiguration of ethics, agency, and truth. His silence is not passive resistance—it is an agential rupture, a performative cut that exposes the violence of legal formalism and affirms the possibility of justice beyond representation.

“Justice is not about right response to a wrong but about the reconfiguring of the very conditions of response.” — Karen Barad

Dr. Chen’s silence reconfigures those conditions. It is not the end of speech—it is the beginning of a different kind of listening.
