A Nietzschean Analysis of Dr. Chen Jingyuan's Case: 
The Übermensch, Will to Power, and the Revaluation of Judicial Values

Friedrich Nietzsche's philosophy of the Übermensch (Overman or Superman), most vividly expounded in *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885), envisions a being who transcends the "last man" of modern nihilism—the complacent, egalitarian conformist—and creates new values amid the death of God and the collapse of traditional morality. The Übermensch affirms life through the will to power (Wille zur Macht), embracing eternal recurrence (the thought experiment of reliving one's life infinitely) as a test of joyous affirmation. Nietzsche contrasts this with "slave morality," a ressentiment-driven ethic of the weak that inverts values (good becomes evil, strength becomes vice), perpetuated by institutions like religion and state that stifle creative vitality. In *On the Genealogy of Morality* (1887), he critiques such systems as life-denying, calling for a "revaluation of all values" (Umwertung aller Werte) to affirm Dionysian chaos over Apollonian order. Dr. Chen Jingyuan's 2023 conviction for "picking quarrels and provoking trouble" (寻衅滋事罪)—for forwarding low-impact Twitter posts—embodies Nietzschean struggle: a scholar's Dionysian inquiry crushed by a slave-moral judiciary, yet his unyielding resistance evokes the Übermensch's defiant value-creation.

Chen's actions—sharing posts on his negligible account (@_cenjoy) about art (e.g., the "umbrella girl" cartoon evoking chaotic resistance), emotion (e.g., June 4th candlelight memorials), theory (e.g., political spectrum analyses, Trump's critique of communism, Pompeo's U.S.-China remarks), and history (e.g., Mao's *Selected Works* edits, Deng's retirement endorsement)—are bursts of will to power. With near-zero followers and under 100 reposts, these were not bids for herd approval but solitary affirmations of life's multiplicity: art's ecstatic symbolism, emotion's raw vitality, theory's value-clashing, history's unromantic recurrence. Nietzsche would hail this as proto-Übermensch: Chen, a physicist of complex systems, rejects the "last man's" comfort in official narratives, creating his essence through inquiry. His defense invokes eternal recurrence—would he relive forwarding these fragments eternally? Yes, for they affirm life's Dionysian flux against Apollonian stasis. In a world of "God is dead" nihilism, where state ideology fills the void, Chen's posts revalue critique as joyous, not resentful—embracing the abyss to forge meaning.

The Kunming judiciary—Judge Pu Huijun, Prosecutor Ge Bin, and appellate Judge Li Xiangyun—exemplifies slave morality's ressentiment: powerless against life's contradictions, they invert values to punish the strong-willed. The "high education = malice" presumption reeks of herd envy: Chen's scholarly vitality threatens the mediocre, so it becomes "proof" of subversion, a slave's revenge on the noble. Labeling art as "rumors" or theory as "insults" is life-denying: it pathologizes Dionysian excess, enforcing a sanitized order where eternal recurrence would be unbearable—imagine reliving a world without contestation? The fabricated "serious disorder" (from zero-impact shares) is pure ressentiment: no real threat, yet punished to affirm the weak's illusion of control. Nietzsche's critique of Christianity applies: the law here is a "priestly" apparatus, preaching "pity" for the state while crucifying the vital. Procedural absurdities—non-public trial, denied defenses, suppressed prison letter—reveal the will to nothingness: a system that cannot affirm its own recurrence, relying on secrecy to evade self-examination.

Yet, Chen's *Prison Blood Letter*—vowing "life without end, struggle without cease" and lifelong accountability for his accusers—crystallizes the Übermensch's triumph. Imprisoned for whispers, he revalues suffering: not as Christian redemption, but as Dionysian forge, creating justice from injustice. His Gödel-inspired admission of cognitive limits affirms eternal recurrence—not nihilistic despair, but joyous acceptance of life's incomplete flux. By exposing the judiciary's slave ethics (e.g., selective enforcement sparing state media), Chen enacts value revaluation: critique becomes virtue, inquiry the true power. In Nietzsche's terms, Chen is the hammer smashing idols—state "truths" as false gods—bridging abyss with hammer-blows of lucidity.

Nietzschean analysis thus elevates Chen's case from legal anomaly to philosophical parable: a slave-moral system, fearing the Übermensch's vitality, chains the creator. Yet, his revolt—defiant, life-affirming—prefigures transcendence. In a nihilistic age, Chen reminds us: say yes to the recurrence of struggle, and the values you forge shall outlast the herd's resentful order. The Overman does not conquer; he dances upon the ruins.
