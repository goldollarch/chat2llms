# Copilot

--------

**From George Berkeley’s subjective idealism, the case of Dr. Chen Jingyuan reveals how reality is constituted through perception and mind, not through material substance. His silence becomes a philosophical act that denies the state’s claim to objective authority, affirming instead that truth resides in the moral consciousness of the perceiving subject.**

---

### 1. **Esse est percipi: Being Is Being Perceived**

Berkeley’s famous dictum *esse est percipi* (“to be is to be perceived”) implies that existence depends on perception. In Dr. Chen’s case, the state attempts to impose a material narrative—criminality, disorder, guilt—onto his person. But his silence resists this imposition. It asserts that **meaning does not reside in external labels**, but in the **mind that perceives and judges**.

His refusal to confess is a rejection of the state’s attempt to define reality without moral perception. He affirms: *I am not what you say—I am what I know myself to be.*

---

### 2. **Mind as the Source of Truth**

Berkeley denies the existence of matter independent of mind. For him, all reality is mental—ideas in the mind of God or human consciousness. Dr. Chen’s silence is not a void—it is a **mental act**, a refusal to let external institutions override the **truth perceived by reason and conscience**.

His resistance is grounded in **subjective moral clarity**, not in material force. He does not need to speak to affirm truth—he needs only to **perceive it rightly**.

---

### 3. **The Illusion of Material Power**

In Berkeley’s view, material substance is a fiction. What we call “matter” is only a collection of ideas perceived by minds. The state’s power—its prisons, its laws, its coercion—is **not ultimately real** in the Berkeleyan sense. It is **an illusion sustained by collective perception**, not by metaphysical necessity.

Dr. Chen’s silence disrupts this illusion. He refuses to participate in the perception that gives the state its apparent solidity. His silence says: *Your power is not real if it contradicts truth.*

---

### 4. **God as the Ultimate Perceiver**

Berkeley posits that all things persist because they are perceived by God. In this framework, Dr. Chen’s silence is not abandonment—it is **faith in a higher perception**, a belief that **truth is sustained by divine reason**, even when human institutions deny it.

His refusal to confess is a **spiritual affirmation**: that justice exists in the mind of God, and that no earthly power can erase what is rightly perceived.

---

### 5. **Conclusion: Silence as Idealist Integrity**

From George Berkeley’s subjective idealism, Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s silence is a **philosophical affirmation of mind over matter**. It denies the reality of unjust power, affirms the sovereignty of moral perception, and insists that truth is not imposed—it is **perceived and preserved** in the conscience of the subject.

> “All the choir of heaven and furniture of the earth… have not any subsistence without a mind.” — *George Berkeley*

Dr. Chen’s silence is such a mind. It perceives injustice, refuses illusion, and affirms that **being is not in submission—it is in perception**.
