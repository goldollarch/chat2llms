**From the philosophical standpoint of Sextus Empiricus and Pyrrhonian skepticism, the case of Dr. Chen Jingyuan exemplifies the ethical and epistemic power of withholding assent (*epoché*) in the face of dogmatic authority. His silence is not a failure to respond—it is a principled suspension of judgment, a refusal to affirm or deny under conditions where truth has been distorted by coercion and ideology.**

---

### 1. **Epoché: Suspension as Moral Clarity**

Sextus Empiricus taught that when conflicting claims cannot be resolved with certainty, the wise response is *epoché*—suspension of judgment. Dr. Chen’s refusal to confess is a form of **ethical epoché**. He does not affirm guilt, nor does he engage in futile self-defense within a system that has already predetermined his fate. His silence is a **philosophical refusal to participate in dogma**, a gesture that says: *Where truth is corrupted, I will not pretend to know.*

This is not indecision—it is **moral clarity through epistemic restraint**.

---

### 2. **Isostheneia: Equal Weight of Opposing Claims**

Sextus emphasized *isostheneia*, the equal strength of opposing arguments, as a reason to suspend judgment. In Dr. Chen’s case, the state presents accusations based on vague and politically charged terms, while his own writings and conduct reflect legal reasoning and ethical concern. His silence highlights the **imbalance of discourse**, exposing how one side wields power while the other appeals to reason.

His refusal to speak is a **skeptical exposure of asymmetry**, a demand that truth not be decided by force.

---

### 3. **Ataraxia: Peace Through Non-Dogmatism**

The goal of Pyrrhonian skepticism is *ataraxia*—freedom from disturbance through the rejection of dogmatic belief. Dr. Chen’s silence is not passive—it is a **search for inner peace**, a refusal to be disturbed by a system that demands ideological submission. He does not speak because speech, under duress, would violate his intellectual and moral equilibrium.

His silence is a **shield of serenity**, forged in the fire of principled doubt.

---

### 4. **Against the Pretension of Knowledge**

Sextus Empiricus critiqued philosophers and institutions that claimed to possess certain knowledge. The legal system that prosecutes Dr. Chen operates under such pretension—asserting guilt without transparent reasoning. His silence is a **skeptical rebuke**, a refusal to validate claims that lack epistemic grounding.

He reminds us that **true inquiry begins where certainty ends**.

---

### 5. **Conclusion: Silence as Skeptical Integrity**

From Sextus Empiricus’s Pyrrhonian perspective, Dr. Chen Jingyuan’s silence is a **philosophical act of integrity**. It suspends judgment in a system that demands confession without proof, exposes the imbalance of power and reason, and affirms the dignity of doubt in the face of dogma. His refusal to speak is not weakness—it is **the strength of one who knows that truth cannot be forced, and that peace lies in principled restraint**.

> “Skepticism is an ability to place appearances and judgments in opposition… and thus to achieve tranquility.” — *Sextus Empiricus*

Dr. Chen’s silence is such an ability. It does not resolve—it **resists**. It does not declare—it **withholds**. And in doing so, it becomes a mirror in which the pretensions of power are revealed.
