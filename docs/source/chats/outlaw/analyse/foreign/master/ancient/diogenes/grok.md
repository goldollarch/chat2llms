Diogenes of Sinope (c. 412/404–323 BCE), the founder of Cynicism ("dog-like" philosophy), lived as a radical ascetic in a barrel, embodying his core ideas: *kata phusin* (living in accordance with nature), rejecting societal conventions and material possessions for self-sufficiency (*autarkeia*); shameless truth-telling (*anaideia*) to expose hypocrisy, as in his lantern search for "a human being" amid pretenders; and cosmopolitan virtue (*kosmopolitês*) as citizenship of the world, scorning artificial hierarchies like wealth or power. Diogenes mocked Alexander the Great with "Stand out of my sunlight," prioritizing inner freedom over external dominance.
